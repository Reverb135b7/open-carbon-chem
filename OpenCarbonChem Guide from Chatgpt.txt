# MASTER PROJECT HANDOVER — OpenCarbonChem

## 1. Project Identity

**Project Name:** OpenCarbonChem

**Full Title:** Open-Source Carbon Accounting and Sustainability Analytics Engine for Chemical and Process Industries.

**Objective:** Develop a modular, open-source Python application for industrial greenhouse gas accounting, Product Carbon Footprint (PCF), Scope 1, 2 and 3 emissions calculations, uncertainty analysis and decarbonisation scenario modelling.

The project must demonstrate the integration of:
- Chemical engineering principles.
- Industrial sustainability.
- Life Cycle Assessment.
- Product Carbon Footprint.
- Corporate GHG accounting.
- Python software engineering.
- Environmental data analytics.

This is intended to be a substantial engineering portfolio project suitable for GitHub publication, CV inclusion, ESG consulting applications and potential academic research.

## 2. Primary Development Objective

Build a functional, reproducible and auditable carbon accounting platform.

The application must support two independent accounting workflows:

**A. Corporate GHG Accounting**
- Scope 1 direct emissions.
- Scope 2 purchased energy emissions.
- All 15 Scope 3 categories.
- Organisational boundaries and reporting periods.
- Annual GHG inventory and emissions intensity.

**B. Product Carbon Footprint**
- Product definition.
- Functional unit.
- System boundary.
- Life-cycle inventory.
- Raw material emissions.
- Manufacturing energy.
- Transportation.
- Packaging.
- Waste.
- Allocation.

These workflows must share a common calculation engine without double-counting emissions.

## 5. Calculation Requirements

Fundamental emissions calculation:

E = Activity Data × Emission Factor

Gas-specific CO2e calculation:

E_CO2e = Sum(E_g × GWP_g)

Product carbon footprint:

PCF = Total attributable life-cycle emissions / Functional unit output

Scenario reduction:

Reduction = Baseline emissions − Scenario emissions

Percentage reduction:

Reduction (%) = (Reduction / Baseline emissions) × 100

Ensure all calculations use dimensionally compatible units.

Never silently apply an incompatible unit conversion.

Maintain separate records for activity data, emission factors, conversion factors, GWP values and calculation results.

## 6. Emission Factor Database

Create a structured emission factor database.

Each record must contain:

- Unique factor ID.
- Activity category.
- Fuel, material or energy type.
- Factor value.
- Factor unit.
- Gas coverage.
- Geographic applicability.
- Reporting year.
- Source organisation.
- Source URL or document reference.
- Publication/version information.
- Uncertainty information, where available.
- Methodological notes.

Use authoritative sources such as IPCC, applicable national grid databases, government conversion-factor datasets and appropriately licensed LCA databases.

Do not invent emission factors or claim illustrative values are official.

Clearly distinguish measured primary activity data from secondary emission factors.

Do not redistribute proprietary databases without permission.

## 7. Corporate GHG Accounting

Implement the GHG Protocol framework.

Scope 1:
- Stationary combustion.
- Mobile combustion.
- Fugitive emissions.
- Process emissions.

Scope 2:
- Purchased electricity.
- Purchased steam.
- Purchased heating and cooling.
- Location-based and market-based methods where applicable.

Scope 3:
Implement all 15 GHG Protocol categories, including category applicability, data requirements and exclusions.

Ensure the software supports organisational boundary documentation and reporting-year comparisons.
